02 / BRAND SYSTEM

Voice & Tone

LevelUp Media speaks like an intelligent sports insider. Direct, confident, and number-centric — never corporate for the sake of sounding corporate.

The Five Voice Principles

Every piece of written copy — from email introductions to deck headlines and web copy — must embody these five characteristics:

01 / DIRECT, NOT DECORATED

Headline with short, punchy statements.

Strip away adjective clutter, long intro phrases, and corporate filler. Tell the reader what we do and why it matters in under 10 words.

02 / CONFIDENT, NUMBER-CENTRIC

Numbers don't lie. Lead with metrics.

Anchor claims with verifiable data points: 400M+ users, 150+ brands, booked versus delivered. Facts build confidence faster than marketing promises.

03 / MYTH-BREAKING, NOT DEFENSIVE

Expose agency inefficiencies directly.

Identify the gaps in traditional sports marketing (5 contracts, 5 rate cards) clearly and present our ecosystem as the natural upgrade.

04 / CONSULTATIVE, NOT TRANSACTIONAL

Build around client objectives, not rate cards.

We advise on sports culture, audience behavior, and campaign design — we don't just sell ad impressions off a menu.

05 / INSIDE SPORTS CULTURE

Speak fan language with restraint.

Use real sports terminology (rights, match broadcasts, creator feeds) without sounding like a noisy sports betting app or gaming advert.

Say / Don't Say Matrix

Compare these real examples to understand the difference between LevelUp voice and generic agency speak:

✓ SAY THIS
"We run all five as one."
Direct statement explaining our connected ecosystem in six words.
✕ AVOID THIS
"We pride ourselves on offering an integrated approach to campaign delivery."
Generic corporate cliché with zero memorability.
✓ SAY THIS
"Numbers don't lie."
Establishes our data-centric proof mindset immediately.
✕ AVOID THIS
"We provide innovative solutions for brands looking to engage audiences."
Meaningless buzzwords ("innovative solutions", "engage audiences").
✓ SAY THIS
"One partner. All solutions."
Clear value proposition contrasting against multi-vendor hassle.
✕ AVOID THIS
"We are passionate about creating meaningful connections."
Overly emotional agency marketing trope.
✓ SAY THIS
"Sport isn't one door."
Inside observation about modern fan consumption habits.
✕ AVOID THIS
"Our 360-degree holistic marketing capabilities empower stakeholders."
Stuffy, jargon-heavy sentence that says nothing concrete.
✓ SAY THIS
"Built, not bought."
Three words capturing our owned access positioning.
✕ AVOID THIS
"We leverage strategic synergistic acquisition models."
Aggressive corporate jargon.
